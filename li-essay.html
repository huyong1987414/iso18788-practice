Exam A — Case study: Tigris Shield Security Services (TSS) — Basra

Tigris Shield Security Services (TSS) is a fictional Iraqi-registered private security company headquartered in Basra, licensed by the Ministry of Interior to provide armed and unarmed security services. It employs about 640 people: 520 local guards (mostly recruited from communities around the oil fields), 70 mobile-team operators and drivers, 25 close-protection officers (a mix of Iraqi and third-country nationals) and 25 managers and support staff.

TSS provides three services to international oil companies (IOCs) and their contractors in Basra Governorate: (1) static guarding of two oilfield camps and a pipeline pumping station; (2) armed convoy escort of client staff and materials between the camps, Basra International Airport and the port of Umm Qasr; and (3) close protection (CP) for visiting expatriate executives. Around 15% of the static guard force at the pumping station is supplied by a local subcontractor owned by a tribal sheikh, which TSS was asked to use by the client to maintain community relations.

Last year, the largest client (an IOC) informed TSS that renewal of its five-year contract will require TSS to be certified to ISO 18788 within 12 months and to demonstrate alignment with the International Code of Conduct for Private Security Service Providers (ICoC) and the Voluntary Principles on Security and Human Rights. The CEO has appointed the Operations Director as SOMS project sponsor and hired you as Lead Implementer.

During your first two weeks you collect the following information: there is a 'Security Policy' signed four years ago by a former CEO; standard operating procedures (SOPs) exist for convoy movements but are different in each camp, and some are only in English although most guards read Arabic only; the rules for the use of force (RUF) are a one-page document copied from a former military unit; weapons are issued from three armouries with paper logbooks; and there is no formal risk assessment — mobile team leaders decide routes 'from experience'.

Recent events include: (a) a convoy escort team fired warning shots at a vehicle that approached a convoy at speed near Az Zubayr — nobody was injured, but the incident was reported to the client three days later and not to the authorities; (b) a peaceful protest by unemployed young men from a nearby village blocked a camp gate for six hours; (c) two guards were hospitalised with heat stroke in July when temperatures exceeded 50 °C, and a dust storm forced a convoy to stop on the highway for two hours; (d) an anonymous message on social media claimed that subcontracted guards pay a 'fee' to the sheikh to keep their jobs.

The CEO wants the SOMS to cover 'everything we do in Iraq', but the HR and finance functions are in a shared-service office in Erbil owned by the parent company. Management have asked for a project plan, a clear scope, and a measurable way to show the client that the SOMS works.

Questions

Question 1 (5 points · Domain 1: Fundamental principles and concepts of a security operations management system (SOMS) · Comprehension / application / analysis)

ISO 18788 is based on the seven quality management principles. Choose any FIVE of these principles and, for each one, give one concrete example of how it should be applied in TSS's security operations (one point per principle with an appropriate example).

Show model answer & marking guide
1. Customer focus — define, with the IOC, the security service requirements (manning levels, response times, reporting deadlines such as reporting an escalation-of-force incident within hours, not three days) and monitor client and community satisfaction. 2. Leadership — the CEO issues a new, signed SOMS policy that commits to human rights and the ICoC, provides resources and personally chairs the management review. 3. Engagement of people — involve guards and team leaders in drafting Arabic SOPs and route risk reviews; recognise good reporting of near-misses. 4. Process approach — manage convoy escort as a defined process (request → threat/route assessment → briefing → movement → debrief/report) with owners, inputs, outputs and controls, instead of each camp doing it differently. 5. Improvement — use incidents such as the warning-shot event and the heat-stroke cases as triggers for root-cause analysis and corrective action. 6. Evidence-based decision making — base route selection, manning and weapon-holding decisions on recorded incident data and documented risk assessments, not 'experience' alone. 7. Relationship management — manage the tribal subcontractor, the client, the police and local communities as interested parties: due diligence, contractual human-rights clauses, community liaison.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 5 pts · Five principles correctly named, each with a relevant TSS-specific example (1 point each)
  • Note · Note: principle named without an example applied to TSS = 0.5 point; generic textbook example not linked to the scenario = max 0.5

ISO 18788 clauses: Introduction (management principles / process approach); 4.1; 5.1; 9.1; 10

Question 2 (5 points · Domain 2: Security operations management system (SOMS) and ISO 18788 requirements · Comprehension / application / analysis)

In the style of the handbook's sample question, provide an action plan with at least two concrete actions for TSS to conform to EACH of the following requirements: (a) clause 5.2 Policy; (b) clause 7.5 Documented information (with regard to the convoy SOPs and the RUF).

Show model answer & marking guide
(a) 5.2 Policy • Draft a new security operations management policy appropriate to TSS's purpose and context, including commitments to comply with legal requirements and respect human rights (ICoC, Voluntary Principles), to satisfy applicable requirements, and to continually improve the SOMS; provide a framework for setting objectives. • Have the current CEO approve and sign it, translate it into Arabic, communicate it in induction and toolbox talks, make it available to interested parties (client, subcontractor) and review it at planned intervals (e.g., annually at management review). (b) 7.5 Documented information • Create one controlled set of convoy SOPs and a rewritten RUF, with unique identifiers, version, author, reviewer/approver and date, in Arabic and English. • Establish a document control procedure: approval before issue, a master list, withdrawal of obsolete copies from the camps, change log, controlled distribution to vehicles and posts, retention and protection of records (e.g., armoury logs, incident reports) against loss and unauthorised alteration.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2.5 pts · (a) Two relevant actions for 5.2 (content of policy incl. commitments; approval, communication, availability, review)
  • 2.5 pts · (b) Two relevant actions for 7.5 (identification/format/review & approval; control of distribution, version, retention, obsolete copies, language)

ISO 18788 clauses: 5.2; 7.5 (7.5.1–7.5.3); 7.4

Question 3 (5 points · Domain 3: Planning the SOMS implementation · Comprehension / application / analysis)

The CEO wants the scope to be 'everything we do in Iraq'. Draft a justified SOMS scope statement for TSS that addresses organizational, service and physical boundaries, and explain how the Erbil shared-service functions and the tribal subcontractor should be treated.

Show model answer & marking guide
Proposed scope: "The security operations management system of Tigris Shield Security Services applies to the planning, management and delivery of static guarding, armed convoy escort and close protection services for oil and gas clients in Basra Governorate, Iraq, managed from the Basra headquarters, including the three armouries, two oilfield camps, the pumping station and the convoy routes between the camps, Basra International Airport and Umm Qasr port." Justification: the scope must consider the external/internal issues (4.1) and interested-party requirements (4.2), be available as documented information and state any exclusions with justification. It is aligned with the client requirement and the services actually delivered; vague wording such as 'everything in Iraq' cannot be verified. Erbil HR/finance: they are outside the physical boundary but their processes (recruitment vetting, payroll, training records) affect SOMS conformity, so they are treated as externally provided/controlled processes with defined interfaces and controls (service-level agreement, records access) — they cannot be used to exclude vetting or competence requirements. Subcontractor: its guards deliver services within scope, so it remains within TSS's responsibility: selection, due diligence, contractual flow-down of the RUF, human-rights and ICoC requirements, monitoring and audit.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2.5 pts · Scope statement covers services (1), locations/physical boundaries (1) and organizational boundary (0.5)
  • 1 pts · Justification linked to 4.1/4.2 and documented/verifiable scope
  • 0.75 pts · Erbil functions treated as interfaces/outsourced processes still controlled
  • 0.75 pts · Subcontractor remains in scope / controlled via contract and monitoring

ISO 18788 clauses: 4.3; 4.4; 4.1; 4.2; 8.1 (control of outsourced processes / subcontractors)

Question 4 (5 points · Domain 3: Planning the SOMS implementation · Comprehension / application / analysis)

Using the information in the scenario, identify FIVE risks that TSS should record in its first risk assessment (one per service line at least, plus one climate-related risk). For each, state the likely consequence and one proposed treatment.

Show model answer & marking guide
1. Convoy escort — escalation of force against a civilian vehicle approaching at speed (consequence: death/injury of civilians, human-rights violation, criminal liability, loss of contract). Treatment: clear graduated RUF and vehicle-approach drills, warning devices, training and scenario rehearsal, immediate incident reporting. 2. Static guarding — community protest blocking the camp gate (consequence: interruption of client operations, risk of violent confrontation). Treatment: community liaison/grievance mechanism, de-escalation training, coordination with police, contingency plan. 3. Close protection — attack or kidnap of an expatriate executive (consequence: harm to principal, reputational and contractual damage). Treatment: advance route recce, threat assessment per movement, vetted drivers, comms checks, emergency rally points. 4. Subcontractor — exploitation of workers ('fees' to the sheikh), unvetted guards (consequence: human-rights abuse, insider threat, client and ICoC non-compliance). Treatment: due diligence, direct payment of wages into bank accounts, contract clauses, independent welfare interviews, whistle-blower channel. 5. Climate/environment (Amd 1:2024) — extreme heat and dust storms (consequence: heat illness, reduced alertness, convoys stranded on highway). Treatment: shaded posts, water/rest cycles, shift changes, weather monitoring, convoy stop/hold-points and go/no-go criteria. Other acceptable: weapons loss/theft from paper-based armouries; late reporting to authorities; language barrier (English-only SOPs).

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2.5 pts · Five relevant risks drawn from the scenario, incl. coverage of the service lines and a climate-related risk (0.5 each)
  • 2.5 pts · Plausible consequence and appropriate treatment for each risk (0.5 each)

ISO 18788 clauses: 6.1; 4.1 (climate change, Amd 1:2024); 4.2; 8.1

Question 5 (10 points · Domain 4: Implementing a SOMS · Evaluation)

Evaluate TSS's current rules for the use of force (a one-page document copied from a former military unit) in light of the warning-shot incident near Az Zubayr. Explain why it is not adequate for ISO 18788, and propose the elements of a new use-of-force process (documentation, training, reporting and review) that TSS should implement.

Show model answer & marking guide
Evaluation: a military document is designed for armed conflict and rules of engagement, not for a private security provider whose personnel act under domestic law and only in self-defence, defence of others or as specified in the contract. It does not reflect Iraqi law, the client contract, the ICoC or human-rights principles (necessity, proportionality, legality, accountability). The incident shows: warning shots were used without clear criteria; no immediate reporting to the client or authorities; no post-incident investigation. This is a nonconformity with clause 8 operational controls and with legal/other requirements (4.2). New process: 1. Documented RUF/use-of-force procedure, legally reviewed: graduated force continuum (presence, verbal warning, visual/audible signals, physical barriers, defensive driving, less-lethal where lawful, lethal force only as a last resort to protect life); explicit position on warning shots (generally prohibited or strictly limited); vehicle-approach procedure; weapons authorisation per person. 2. Translate into Arabic and issue as controlled documents; RUF cards for each operator. 3. Competence: initial and refresher training with scenario-based drills, weapons qualification and documented assessment; only authorised and qualified personnel carry specific weapons. 4. Reporting: every use of force (including warning shots and weapon display) reported immediately to the operations room, written report within 24 h (or the contract/legal deadline), notification to client and to competent authorities as required by law. 5. Investigation: independent review of each incident, root cause, disciplinary/corrective action, support for affected persons and remedy where needed. 6. Monitoring: KPI on use-of-force incidents, trend analysis, input to management review; periodic review of RUF after incidents or legal changes.

Marking guide (10 points)

  • 3 pts · Evaluation: explains why military ROE are unsuitable (legal basis, self-defence, proportionality/necessity, human rights/ICoC)
  • 1 pts · Links incident to specific gaps (warning shots, late reporting, no investigation)
  • 2 pts · Documented, legally reviewed graduated RUF incl. warning shots & vehicle approach
  • 1.5 pts · Training/competence and authorisation of armed personnel
  • 1.5 pts · Reporting and notification (client, authorities) with timescales
  • 1 pts · Investigation, corrective action and review/monitoring

ISO 18788 clauses: 8.1; Clause 8 (use-of-force requirements); 4.2 (legal and other requirements); 7.2; 7.5; 10.1

Question 6 (5 points · Domain 4: Implementing a SOMS · Evaluation)

Most guards read Arabic only and many work night shifts at remote posts. Recommend and justify an awareness and communication plan for the new SOMS, including the whistle-blower policy, that will be effective for this workforce.

Show model answer & marking guide
• What: SOMS policy, RUF, human-rights obligations/ICoC, incident and grievance reporting, heat-stress controls, the whistle-blower policy (confidential, no retaliation). • How (suited to Arabic-speaking, shift-based guards): Arabic posters and pocket cards; short toolbox talks at shift handover led by supervisors; video/voice messages via WhatsApp/radio briefings; role-play scenarios; quizzes to check understanding. Include subcontracted guards. • Whistle-blower channel: multiple anonymous routes (hotline/WhatsApp number answered by someone outside the line chain, locked box at camps, e-mail to compliance/client), clear non-retaliation statement, case handling and feedback; communicate it at induction and every quarter. The 'fee' allegation shows why this channel is needed. • Who/when: communication matrix with owner, audience, frequency, method; external communication to client, community and authorities defined. • Effectiveness: attendance records, test results, number of reports received, spot interviews by supervisors and internal auditors.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 1 pts · Content of awareness (policy, RUF, reporting, contribution & implications of not conforming)
  • 1.5 pts · Methods adapted to language/shift/remote posts, incl. subcontractors
  • 1.5 pts · Whistle-blower channel: confidential/anonymous, non-retaliation, handling
  • 1 pts · Communication plan elements (what, when, with whom, how, who) and effectiveness check

ISO 18788 clauses: 7.3; 7.4 (incl. 7.4.5 whistle-blower policy); 7.2

Question 7 (5 points · Domain 4: Implementing a SOMS · Evaluation)

The client asked TSS to use the tribal sheikh's company for 15% of the pumping-station guards, and an allegation of 'fees' has appeared on social media. Evaluate the situation and recommend the controls TSS must implement for this subcontractor.

Show model answer & marking guide
Evaluation: TSS remains responsible for services delivered by the subcontractor; the client request does not transfer responsibility. The allegation indicates possible forced-labour/exploitation and corruption risks, insider-threat and vetting risks, and a reputational risk for TSS and the IOC. It must be treated as a potential human-rights incident — investigated promptly and confidentially, not ignored. Controls: • Due diligence before/at renewal: legal registration and licensing, ownership/conflict of interest, past human-rights record, ability to meet vetting and training requirements. • Contract flow-down: ISO 18788/ICoC requirements, RUF, no recruitment fees, payment of wages directly to guards, right to audit, termination clauses. • Vetting and training of each subcontracted guard to TSS standards; TSS supervisors on site. • Monitoring: periodic audits, confidential worker interviews, grievance/whistle-blower access for subcontracted guards, KPIs. • Communicate the outcome to the client and agree remediation; escalate to management review.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2 pts · Evaluation: TSS remains accountable; identifies human-rights/corruption/vetting risks; investigate allegation
  • 1.5 pts · Due diligence and contractual flow-down incl. right to audit and no-fee clauses
  • 1.5 pts · Vetting/training/supervision and ongoing monitoring incl. grievance access

ISO 18788 clauses: 8.1 (outsourced processes / subcontractors); 4.2; 6.1; 7.4.5; 8 (human rights)

Question 8 (5 points · Domain 4: Implementing a SOMS · Evaluation)

Weapons are issued from three armouries using paper logbooks. Propose and justify a process for the control of weapons and ammunition that would satisfy ISO 18788 and the likely expectations of the Ministry of Interior and the client.

Show model answer & marking guide
• Policy/procedure: documented weapons and ammunition management procedure covering acquisition, licensing, storage, issue/return, transport, maintenance, loss/theft and disposal, in line with Iraqi licensing law and contract. • Registration: master register of every weapon (serial number, licence, assigned location), ammunition stock and authorised users. • Issue/return: issue only to trained, authorised and licensed personnel against signature (or electronic system/barcode), with round counts in and out; reconcile at end of every shift; two-person control of armoury keys. • Storage and security: secure armoury, access control, CCTV/alarms where feasible, separation of weapons and ammunition. • Checks: daily reconciliation, monthly inventory by a person independent of the armourer, unannounced audits; any discrepancy reported immediately and investigated; loss reported to authorities. • Records: logs retained as documented information and reviewed in internal audit; KPIs (discrepancies, maintenance overdue). Justification: reduces risk of misuse, loss and diversion, provides evidence for licensing authorities and the client.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 1 pts · Documented procedure aligned with legal/licensing requirements
  • 1.5 pts · Register, authorisation and issue/return controls with reconciliation
  • 1 pts · Physical security / segregation of duties / key control
  • 1.5 pts · Inventory checks, discrepancy reporting & investigation, records

ISO 18788 clauses: 8.1; Clause 8 (weapons/equipment controls); 4.2; 7.1; 7.5

Question 9 (10 points · Domain 5: Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation of a SOMS · Evaluation)

Management asked for 'a measurable way to show the client that the SOMS works'. Design a measurement programme for TSS: propose at least SIX indicators (covering strategic, tactical and operational levels), with a definition, data source, frequency and target for each, and explain how the results will be analysed, reported and used.

Show model answer & marking guide
Indicators (examples): Strategic (quarterly, to CEO/board and client): 1. Number of use-of-force incidents per 100,000 man-hours; source: incident register; target: downward trend, 100% investigated. 2. Client and community satisfaction index; source: quarterly client survey + grievance log; target ≥ 4/5, all grievances acknowledged within 7 days. Tactical (monthly, to Operations Director): 3. % of personnel with valid RUF/weapons training and vetting; source: HR/training records; target 100%. 4. % of incidents reported to client within the contract deadline (e.g., 24 h) and to authorities as required; source: incident reports; target 100%. 5. Corrective actions closed on time; source: CAPA log; target ≥ 90%. Operational (daily/weekly, to camp/convoy managers): 6. Armoury reconciliation discrepancies; source: armoury logs; target 0. 7. Convoy movements with documented route risk assessment and briefing; source: movement files; target 100%. 8. Heat-stress cases / posts with water-shade-rest compliance; source: supervisor checklists, medic log; target 0 heat-stroke cases. Analysis & use: each indicator documented (owner, formula, data source, frequency, target, reporting format); data validated; trends analysed monthly; dashboards for each level; deviations trigger root-cause analysis and corrective action; results reported to management review and selected results shared with the client in a monthly report; indicators reviewed annually for relevance.

Marking guide (10 points)

  • 3 pts · At least six relevant indicators covering strategic, tactical and operational levels (0.5 each, max 3)
  • 3 pts · Each indicator has definition/formula, data source, frequency and target
  • 2 pts · Analysis method: trends, validation, owner, dashboards per level
  • 2 pts · Use of results: reporting to management/client, corrective action, input to management review

ISO 18788 clauses: 9.1; 6.2; 9.3; 10.1

Question 10 (10 points · Domain 5: Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation of a SOMS · Evaluation)

Six months into the project, TSS's Operations Director proposes that he will personally perform the first internal audit of the whole SOMS in one week, because 'nobody knows the operations better than me'. Evaluate this proposal and describe how the internal audit programme should be established instead, including how a finding from the warning-shot incident would be written as a nonconformity.

Show model answer & marking guide
Evaluation: internal auditors must be objective and impartial; the Operations Director owns the processes being audited (convoy, static, CP) and is the SOMS sponsor, so he would audit his own work. A single one-week audit of everything may not have sufficient depth, and he may lack auditor competence. The proposal does not conform to 9.2. Audit programme: documented programme (frequency, methods, responsibilities, planning requirements, reporting) based on importance of processes, risk and results of previous audits — higher-risk processes (use of force, weapons, subcontractors, convoy) audited more often. Define criteria and scope of each audit; select competent, independent auditors (trained internal auditors from another function, Erbil staff, or an external consultant); audit plan; opening meeting; evidence collection at camps, armouries and on convoys (interviews in Arabic, record sampling, observation); report results to relevant management; retain documented information; follow up corrective actions. Example nonconformity: Requirement: ISO 18788 clause 8 (use-of-force reporting) and TSS RUF procedure §x require that all use-of-force incidents be reported to the client within 24 h and to the competent authorities. Nonconformity: the warning-shot incident of [date] near Az Zubayr was reported to the client after three days and not reported to the authorities. Evidence: incident report ref. …, e-mail to client dated …, interview with team leader. Grade: major (systemic absence of reporting process with significant human-rights/legal impact) or minor if isolated — with justification.

Marking guide (10 points)

  • 3 pts · Evaluation: lack of objectivity/impartiality, competence, insufficient depth — nonconforms with 9.2
  • 2.5 pts · Risk-based audit programme: frequency, methods, responsibilities, criteria, scope
  • 2 pts · Competent independent auditors and audit conduct/reporting/follow-up
  • 2.5 pts · Nonconformity written with requirement, statement of NC, evidence and grading

ISO 18788 clauses: 9.2; 9.1; 10.1; Clause 8 (use of force)

Question 11 (5 points · Domain 6: Continual improvement of a SOMS · Evaluation)

Two guards suffered heat stroke in July. Apply a root-cause analysis technique of your choice to this event and propose corrective actions, showing how TSS can verify their effectiveness.

Show model answer & marking guide
Technique: 5 Whys / fishbone. Why heat stroke? Guards were exposed to >50 °C for long periods. Why? 12-hour shifts at unshaded posts without scheduled breaks. Why? Post orders and manning plans were designed without considering heat. Why? No risk assessment of climate/heat; climate change not considered as an issue in context. Why? No SOMS risk process and no HSE input to security operations planning. Fishbone categories: people (no heat awareness, not acclimatised), methods (shift pattern), equipment (no shade, insufficient water, black uniforms), environment (extreme heat, dust), management (no monitoring of heat index). Corrective actions: include heat/climate risks in the risk assessment (Amd 1:2024 4.1); heat-stress procedure with work–rest cycles by heat index, water and electrolytes, shaded/air-conditioned posts, lighter uniforms, rotation; train supervisors to recognise symptoms; medic/first-aid response; seasonal readiness check before summer. Effectiveness: monitor heat-related cases and supervisor checklists over the next summer; internal audit of posts; if no recurrence and compliance ≥ 95%, close; otherwise re-analyse. Also consider preventive application to convoys (dust storms).

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2 pts · Correct application of an RCA technique reaching a systemic root cause
  • 2 pts · Corrective actions addressing root cause (not only the symptom)
  • 1 pts · Verification of effectiveness (measure, timeframe, closure criteria)

ISO 18788 clauses: 10.1; 10.2; 6.1; 4.1 (climate change, Amd 1:2024)

Question 12 (5 points · Domain 7: Preparing for a SOMS certification audit · Evaluation)

The CEO wants to book the Stage 2 certification audit next month 'to save time' and asks you whether TSS is ready. Explain the purpose of Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits and list the readiness criteria you would check before advising the CEO.

Show model answer & marking guide
Stage 1: mainly documentation and readiness review — scope, policy, objectives, risk assessment, legal/other requirements, key procedures (RUF, weapons, convoy), evidence that internal audit and management review have been planned/performed; understanding of the site and processes; agree Stage 2 plan. Gaps found at Stage 1 can result in delaying Stage 2. Stage 2: on-site evaluation of implementation and effectiveness — interviews, observation at camps, convoys, armouries; sampling of records; determine whether the SOMS conforms and is effective; nonconformities must be corrected before certification (majors need verified corrections). Readiness criteria: the SOMS has operated for a sufficient period (typically some months) with records; scope/policy/objectives approved; risk assessment and treatment plan completed; key procedures implemented and personnel trained (Arabic); at least one full internal audit cycle and a management review completed; corrective actions for major findings closed; KPIs available; certification body accredited and competent in ISO 18788, contract signed, Stage 1 completed. Advice: TSS cannot skip Stage 1 — book Stage 1 first, then Stage 2 after gaps are closed.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 1.5 pts · Correct purpose of Stage 1
  • 1.5 pts · Correct purpose of Stage 2
  • 2 pts · Readiness criteria (operation period/records, internal audit & management review done, CAPA closed, training, CB selection) and advice

ISO 18788 clauses: 9.2; 9.3; 10.1; ISO/IEC 17021-1 (certification audit process — not an ISO 18788 requirement)

Exam B — Case study: Al-Faw Guardian Risk (AFG) — Maysan & Basra

Al-Faw Guardian Risk (AFG) is a fictional Iraqi private security company with 280 employees, headquartered in Amarah (Maysan Governorate) with a small office in Basra. AFG is already certified to ISO 9001 and ISO 45001, and the General Manager wants an integrated management system that adds ISO 18788 within 10 months.

AFG's services are: (1) static guarding and access control at a gas processing plant operated by a joint venture between an IOC and a state company; (2) mobile security teams that escort seismic survey and pipeline inspection crews through farmland, marshes and tribal areas; and (3) close protection for the joint venture's general manager and visiting delegations. The client contract allows AFG guards to stop and hold intruders at the plant until the police arrive, but not to arrest them.

Findings from your initial interviews: the quality manager believes ISO 18788 is 'just ISO 9001 with weapons'; top management have never formally reviewed security performance; security objectives are not defined — the only figure reported to the client is 'number of guard hours delivered'; recruitment vetting is done by checking a national ID card and a reference letter from a tribal leader; training records exist only for the 40 CP and mobile-team operators; armoured vehicles are maintained by a local garage with no records of brake or tyre inspections.

In the last six months: (a) plant guards caught two young men stealing copper cable inside the perimeter; the guards held them for nine hours in a storage container before the police collected them, and one of them later claimed he was beaten; (b) a mobile team was surrounded by armed men from a tribe that claimed the survey crew was trespassing on their land; the team leader negotiated their exit but the client suspended survey work for three weeks; (c) an employee e-mailed the client's HSE manager alleging that AFG deducts money from guards' salaries for uniforms and that he was threatened after complaining internally; (d) seasonal flooding in the marshes and a summer of severe water shortage increased tension between the communities and the oil industry.

The project budget is limited, and the General Manager asks you to use the existing ISO 9001/45001 system 'as much as possible'.

Questions

Question 1 (5 points · Domain 1: Fundamental principles and concepts of a security operations management system (SOMS) · Comprehension / application / analysis)

The quality manager believes ISO 18788 is 'just ISO 9001 with weapons'. Explain FIVE key concepts or features of ISO 18788 that distinguish a SOMS from a quality management system, using AFG examples.

Show model answer & marking guide
1. Respect for human rights and protection of life — the SOMS must ensure security operations respect human rights and fundamental freedoms (e.g., the treatment of the cable thieves); a QMS only focuses on customer satisfaction. 2. Legal and other requirements specific to security operations — national licensing, weapons laws, international humanitarian law, human-rights law, the ICoC/Montreux Document, contract limits (hold vs arrest). 3. Use-of-force, apprehension/detention controls — requirements for rules on use of force, handling of persons held, and weapons management that have no equivalent in ISO 9001. 4. Risk to internal and external interested parties — risk assessment covers risks to communities, persons affected by operations and the client, not only the organization (e.g., tribal land disputes, community tensions over water). 5. Resilience / management of undesirable and disruptive events — preparing for, responding to and recovering from incidents (team surrounded by armed tribe, flooding). Also acceptable: grievance and whistle-blower mechanisms, vetting and background screening of personnel, community relations, accountability and the operating environment where governance is weak and the rule of law undermined.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2.5 pts · Five distinguishing concepts correctly explained (0.5 each)
  • 2.5 pts · Each illustrated with an AFG example (0.5 each)

Question 2 (5 points · Domain 2: Security operations management system (SOMS) and ISO 18788 requirements · Comprehension / application / analysis)

For EACH of the following clauses of ISO 18788, provide an action plan with at least two concrete actions to ensure AFG's conformity: (a) clause 6.2 Security operations objectives and planning to achieve them; (b) clause 7.2 Competence.

Show model answer & marking guide
(a) 6.2 Objectives • Define measurable security operations objectives consistent with the policy at relevant functions/levels, e.g., 'zero unlawful detentions; 100% of persons held handed to police within 2 hours'; '100% of mobile movements with community liaison check'; 'vetting completed for 100% of guards by month 6'. • For each objective, plan what will be done, resources, responsible person, deadline and how results will be evaluated; communicate, monitor and update; keep documented information. (b) 7.2 Competence • Determine the competence required for each role (guard, supervisor, mobile team leader, CP officer, armourer) including RUF, human rights, first aid, apprehension, de-escalation and Arabic/English needs; create a training matrix. • Train all 280 staff, not only CP/mobile teams; evaluate effectiveness (tests, drills); keep training and qualification records; include background screening (vetting) as a prerequisite for deployment.

  • 2.5 pts · (b) Two relevant actions for 7.2 (determine competence; training/actions, effectiveness evaluation, records)

Question 3 (5 points · Domain 3: Planning the SOMS implementation · Comprehension / application / analysis)

The General Manager wants to reuse the ISO 9001/45001 system 'as much as possible'. Describe how you would perform a gap analysis for AFG and list FIVE gaps you would expect to find based on the scenario.

Show model answer & marking guide
Approach: define criteria (ISO 18788 clauses 4–10, ICoC, Iraqi law, contract); collect information by document review (IMS manual, procedures, contracts), interviews (GM, quality manager, guards, team leaders, client), site observation (plant gate, holding area, vehicles) and record sampling; map each requirement to existing IMS elements (Annex SL common structure allows reuse of policy, document control, internal audit, management review, CAPA); rate each requirement (conform / partial / not implemented) and prioritise by risk; present results and an action plan. Expected gaps: 1. No security objectives or performance indicators beyond guard hours (6.2, 9.1). 2. No management review of security performance (9.3). 3. Inadequate vetting/background screening of personnel (7.2 / clause 8 personnel requirements). 4. No documented procedure for apprehension and handing over of persons; nine-hour holding in a container (clause 8). 5. No whistle-blower/grievance mechanism with non-retaliation (7.4.5). Also: incomplete training records; no maintenance records for armoured vehicles (7.1, 8.1); risk assessment not covering human-rights/community/climate risks (6.1, 4.1).

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2.5 pts · Gap analysis method: criteria, information-gathering techniques, mapping to existing IMS, rating & prioritisation
  • 2.5 pts · Five relevant gaps derived from the scenario (0.5 each)

Question 4 (5 points · Domain 3: Planning the SOMS implementation · Comprehension / application / analysis)

Identify the main interested parties of AFG relevant to the SOMS and their requirements, and explain how climate change should be addressed in AFG's context analysis in line with ISO 18788:2015/Amd 1:2024.

Show model answer & marking guide
Interested parties and requirements (examples): • Client (JV, IOC and state company): contract service levels, hold-not-arrest limit, reporting, ISO 18788 certification. • Iraqi authorities (Ministry of Interior, police, Maysan governorate): licensing, weapons permits, handing over persons, reporting of incidents. • Employees and their families: fair wages without unlawful deductions, safe working conditions, non-retaliation. • Local communities and tribes: respect of land and rights, grievance mechanism, local employment, no harm. • Persons apprehended / members of the public: humane treatment, human rights. • Subcontractors/suppliers (garage), insurers, NGOs/ICoCA, the media. Climate change (Amd 1:2024): in 4.1 AFG shall determine whether climate change is a relevant issue — here it clearly is: floods in the marshes and water shortage affect access routes, raise community tension and conflict over resources, and extreme heat affects personnel. In 4.2, note that relevant interested parties can have climate-related requirements (e.g., client requirements for flood/heat contingency, community concerns over water). These issues then feed into risk assessment (6.1) and operational planning.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 3 pts · At least five relevant interested parties with their requirements
  • 2 pts · Climate change: determine relevance in 4.1, interested-party requirements in 4.2, linked to AFG context and to risk assessment

ISO 18788 clauses: 4.1; 4.2 (incl. Amd 1:2024); 6.1

Question 5 (10 points · Domain 4: Implementing a SOMS · Evaluation)

Evaluate how AFG's guards handled the two cable thieves (held for nine hours in a storage container; later allegation of beating). Propose the procedure AFG should implement for apprehension, search, holding and handover of persons, and the actions to take regarding the beating allegation.

Show model answer & marking guide
Evaluation: the contract allows holding only until the police arrive; nine hours in a storage container (likely hot, unventilated, not designed for persons) suggests arbitrary/unsafe detention, possible degrading treatment and a risk to life; no evidence of a log, medical check or prompt notification; the allegation of beating, if true, is unlawful use of force and a human-rights violation. AFG exposes itself and the client to legal, contractual and reputational consequences. Procedure: 1. Legal basis: define exactly what guards may do under Iraqi law and the contract (hold, not arrest); authorise only trained supervisors. 2. Apprehension: minimum force, verbal commands first; search only when lawful and necessary for safety, by same-gender personnel where possible, with a witness. 3. Holding: designated safe holding area (ventilation, shade, water, toilet, visible to CCTV), never containers; continuous supervision; check for injuries and give first aid. 4. Notification and handover: call police immediately; escalate if not attending within a fixed time (e.g., 1 h, to operations manager/client); handover with signed form, list of property, photos. 5. Records: detention/holding log (times, names, condition, food/water, injuries, people involved) kept as documented information. 6. Training & awareness: human rights, apprehension and handover, RUF scenarios. Allegation: treat as a serious incident — preserve evidence (CCTV, logs, medical records); independent investigation (not by the guards' supervisor); suspend involved guards from duty with weapons pending outcome; inform the client and, where required, authorities; ensure access to remedy for the victim; disciplinary and corrective action; lessons learned to all sites.

Marking guide (10 points)

  • 3 pts · Evaluation: exceeds contract/legal authority, unsafe/degrading holding, lack of records & notification, human-rights risk
  • 2 pts · Procedure: legal basis & authorisation; apprehension/search with minimum force
  • 2 pts · Safe holding conditions, notification/escalation and documented handover
  • 1 pts · Records and training
  • 2 pts · Allegation: evidence preservation, independent investigation, notification, remedy, discipline/corrective action

ISO 18788 clauses: Clause 8 (apprehension, search and detention requirements; use of force; human rights); 4.2; 7.2; 7.5; 10.1

Question 6 (5 points · Domain 4: Implementing a SOMS · Evaluation)

Recruitment vetting at AFG consists of checking a national ID card and a reference letter from a tribal leader. Evaluate this practice and recommend a background screening and vetting process suited to the Iraqi context.

Show model answer & marking guide
Evaluation: an ID and a tribal reference prove identity and social connection, not suitability; they do not detect criminal records, past human-rights abuses, dismissal from security forces, drug use or links to armed groups; tribal letters may create conflicts of interest and insider risk. Recommended process (documented, proportionate to role, in line with privacy law): • Identity and right-to-work verification; verified copies of documents. • Criminal-record check through the competent Iraqi authorities (police clearance) where lawful. • Employment history checks with previous employers, including former army/police service records and reasons for leaving; checks for any record of human-rights violations. • Medical and fitness checks, drug testing where legal; weapons-handling suitability for armed roles. • Interview by HR and security; signed acceptance of code of conduct/RUF. • Re-vetting periodically (e.g., every 2–3 years) and after incidents; apply the same to subcontractors. • Records kept confidentially; decision criteria defined to avoid discrimination.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 1.5 pts · Evaluation of weaknesses (suitability, criminal/human-rights history, conflict of interest/insider risk)
  • 2.5 pts · At least four appropriate screening elements
  • 1 pts · Periodic re-vetting, documentation/confidentiality, application to subcontractors

ISO 18788 clauses: 7.2; Clause 8 (personnel screening / vetting); 4.2; 7.5

Question 7 (5 points · Domain 4: Implementing a SOMS · Evaluation)

An employee e-mailed the client alleging unlawful salary deductions and threats after complaining internally. Evaluate what this reveals about AFG's SOMS and recommend how AFG should handle this case and implement its whistle-blower and grievance arrangements.

Show model answer & marking guide
Evaluation: the employee went outside AFG because there was no trusted internal channel, and he reports retaliation — a failure of the whistle-blower requirement (7.4.5) and of leadership commitment to ethical conduct. Unlawful deductions are also a labour-rights issue (ICoC prohibits such practices) and a legal risk. Handling the case: acknowledge receipt; protect the employee from retaliation; appoint an independent investigator (e.g., compliance officer or external party, not the line manager); review payroll records; inform the client of the process; if confirmed, refund deductions, discipline those who threatened, change payroll practice. Arrangements: documented whistle-blower policy approved by top management; multiple confidential/anonymous channels (hotline, e-mail, box), available also to subcontractors and communities (grievance mechanism); non-retaliation commitment; case management with timelines and feedback; regular communication in Arabic; reporting of statistics (not identities) to management review.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 1.5 pts · Evaluation: no trusted channel, retaliation, leadership/ethics and labour-rights implications
  • 2 pts · Case handling: protection, independent investigation, client communication, remedy
  • 1.5 pts · Whistle-blower/grievance system: policy, confidential channels, non-retaliation, communication, reporting

ISO 18788 clauses: 7.4.5; 5.1; 7.4; 8 (human rights / grievance); 10.1

Question 8 (5 points · Domain 4: Implementing a SOMS · Evaluation)

A mobile team was surrounded by armed tribesmen and survey work was suspended for three weeks. Recommend how AFG should plan and control mobile escort operations in tribal areas to prevent and respond to such undesirable and disruptive events.

Show model answer & marking guide
Planning (prevention): • Pre-movement threat and risk assessment per task (land ownership disputes, tribal relationships, flooding/route conditions, water tensions). • Community/tribal engagement before work starts, together with the client's community-relations team: notification, consent where needed, local liaison officer, grievance mechanism. • Movement plan: routes, timings, go/no-go criteria, communication schedule, check-in points, rally points, medical evacuation; briefings to team and survey crew. • Competence: negotiation and de-escalation training, cultural awareness; RUF emphasising avoiding confrontation. Response & recovery: • Incident response plan with escalation to the operations room, client and authorities; withdrawal procedures; tracking (GPS) and communications redundancy. • Post-incident debrief, report, root cause; restore relations through dialogue; update risk register; exercises/drills to test plans.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2 pts · Risk assessment per movement & community engagement
  • 1.5 pts · Operational control: movement plan, communications, go/no-go, briefings, competence
  • 1.5 pts · Response/escalation, recovery, debrief and testing of plans

ISO 18788 clauses: 8.1; Clause 8 (prevention and management of undesirable or disruptive events / resilience); 6.1; 4.2

Question 9 (10 points · Domain 5: Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation of a SOMS · Evaluation)

The only figure AFG reports to the client is 'number of guard hours delivered'. Evaluate this measure and design a balanced set of indicators and a dashboard for AFG, explaining the difference between operational, tactical and strategic indicators and how each will be used.

Show model answer & marking guide
Evaluation: guard hours measure output/contract volume, not effectiveness, human-rights performance or risk control; it cannot show whether objectives are met (9.1 requires determining what needs to be monitored, methods, when, and when results are analysed). Levels: • Operational (daily/weekly, site supervisors): e.g., posts manned on time (%); vehicle pre-movement checks completed (%); persons held handed to police within 2 h (%); armoury reconciliation discrepancies (0). • Tactical (monthly, operations manager): % staff vetted and trained (target 100%); incidents reported within contract time; corrective actions closed on time; vehicle maintenance compliance; grievances closed within 30 days. • Strategic (quarterly, GM/management review and client): number of human-rights allegations substantiated (target 0); use-of-force incidents trend; client satisfaction; community grievance trend; days of client work lost due to security incidents. Each indicator defined with formula, source, owner, frequency, target and threshold (green/amber/red); dashboard shows trends; results analysed for causes; deviations trigger actions; strategic indicators feed management review and the client report.

Marking guide (10 points)

  • 2 pts · Evaluation of 'guard hours' as output measure, not effectiveness
  • 2 pts · Correct explanation of operational, tactical, strategic levels and audiences
  • 3 pts · At least six relevant indicators across levels with targets
  • 3 pts · Dashboard design: definitions, owners, frequency, thresholds, trend, use in decisions/management review

ISO 18788 clauses: 9.1; 6.2; 9.3

Question 10 (10 points · Domain 5: Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation of a SOMS · Evaluation)

Top management have never formally reviewed security performance. Prepare the agenda (inputs) and expected outputs for AFG's first management review of the SOMS, and explain how you would counsel the General Manager to make it effective rather than a formality.

Show model answer & marking guide
Inputs (agenda): 1. Status of actions from previous reviews (none yet — establish baseline). 2. Changes in external and internal issues: security situation in Maysan/Basra, tribal disputes, flooding and water shortage (climate change), legal changes, client contract changes. 3. Information on SOMS performance: nonconformities and corrective actions; monitoring and measurement results (KPI dashboard); audit results; fulfilment of objectives; incidents including the cable-theft holding and beating allegation, the tribal stand-off; whistle-blower/grievance cases; performance of external providers (garage, any subcontractors). 4. Feedback from interested parties (client, community, employees). 5. Adequacy of resources. 6. Effectiveness of actions to address risks and opportunities. 7. Opportunities for continual improvement. Outputs: decisions on improvement opportunities and any changes to the SOMS (e.g., new apprehension procedure, vetting process), resource needs (budget for training, holding facility, vehicle maintenance), revised objectives, actions with owners and deadlines; documented minutes as evidence. Counsel: GM chairs; scheduled at planned intervals (e.g., quarterly in the first year); pre-read data pack; focus on decisions, not presentations; integrate with the ISO 9001/45001 review but give security its own agenda items; track actions; include human-rights and ethics topics explicitly.

  • 4 pts · Required inputs listed and tailored to AFG (incl. external/internal issues and climate, performance data, feedback, resources, risks, improvement)
  • 3 pts · Outputs: improvement decisions, changes, resources, documented results
  • 3 pts · Counselling: top-management leadership, frequency, data, decisions & follow-up, integration

Question 11 (5 points · Domain 6: Continual improvement of a SOMS · Evaluation)

Armoured vehicles are maintained by a local garage with no records of brake or tyre inspections. Explain the difference between a correction, a corrective action and a preventive approach in this case, and propose an action plan that leads to continual improvement.

Show model answer & marking guide
• Correction (immediate fix): inspect all armoured vehicles now; take any vehicle with defective brakes/tyres out of service; repair before further use. • Corrective action (eliminate the cause to prevent recurrence): root cause — no maintenance requirement in the supplier contract, no vehicle maintenance programme, nobody responsible in AFG. Actions: preventive-maintenance schedule appropriate for armoured vehicles (heavier load); contract requirements and records for the garage; pre-movement driver checklist; fleet manager accountable; verify effectiveness by audit of records and defect rates. • Preventive approach (risk-based thinking): apply the lesson to other external providers and equipment (radios, weapons, medical kits); include equipment failure in the risk register; consider a better-qualified supplier. Continual improvement: track KPIs (maintenance compliance, breakdowns), review in management review, update procedures, PDCA cycle.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2 pts · Correct distinction and example of correction vs corrective action
  • 1.5 pts · Corrective action addresses root cause with effectiveness check
  • 1.5 pts · Preventive / risk-based extension and link to continual improvement

ISO 18788 clauses: 10.1; 10.2; 8.1 (externally provided products and services); 7.1; 6.1

Question 12 (5 points · Domain 7: Preparing for a SOMS certification audit · Evaluation)

During the Stage 2 audit, the external auditor raises a major nonconformity because 'AFG has no procedure for detention operations'. AFG's guards only hold intruders until police arrive and never run detention facilities. Explain how you would counsel AFG to respond to this finding and, if the finding is upheld, how to prepare the action plan.

Show model answer & marking guide
Response: review the finding against the audit criteria and evidence; if detention operations are genuinely outside AFG's services and the scope states this, discuss at the closing meeting with facts — the scope statement, contract clause (hold until police arrive) and the documented apprehension/handover procedure. Ask the auditor to clarify which requirement and evidence the finding is based on. Professional, evidence-based challenge is legitimate; if no agreement, use the certification body's appeal process. However, AFG must show that short-term holding of persons is controlled (procedure, records, training); if those controls are missing, the finding may be justified, possibly reclassified to the correct requirement. If upheld: within the CB's deadline (often 30–90 days for majors), submit a correction and corrective-action plan: root-cause analysis, actions (procedure, training, holding area, records), responsibilities and dates, evidence of implementation; the CB will verify (document review or follow-up audit) before granting certification.

Marking guide (5 points)

  • 2 pts · Evidence-based challenge of the finding (scope, contract, criteria, discussion at closing meeting, appeal)
  • 1 pts · Recognises that holding of persons still needs controls / finding may be valid or reclassified
  • 2 pts · Action plan: root cause, correction, corrective actions, owners/dates, evidence, follow-up audit

ISO 18788 clauses: 4.3; Clause 8 (apprehension and detention requirements); 10.1; certification-body rules (ISO/IEC 17021-1)
